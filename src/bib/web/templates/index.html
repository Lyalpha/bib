What is BPASS?

The Binary Population and Spectral Synthesis code, by J. J. Eldridge (University of Auckland) and E. R. Stanway (University of Warwick), predicts what populations of stars look like as they age — their spectra, colours, ionizing output, supernova rates and chemical yields. Its defining feature is that it follows binary stellar evolution in detail: most massive stars have a companion close enough to interact, and those interactions reshape a population's light in ways single-star models miss. See the tutorial for a gentle introduction, or go straight to the official v2.2 user manual.

🌈

SED Explorer

Plot any simple stellar population at 51 ages from 1 Myr to 100 Gyr. Press play and watch it evolve. Pin snapshots to compare IMFs, metallicities, alpha enhancement and dust reddening side by side.
✨

HR Explorer

Animate individual stellar models across the HR diagram with a live readout of mass, radius and surface composition. A to-scale binary visualizer shows orbits, winds, Roche-lobe overflow and common-envelope events as they happen.
📐

Synthetic photometry

AB magnitudes through bundled filter sets (SDSS, 2MASS, GALEX, LSST, HST, JWST, Gaia…) — or upload your own transmission curve and get magnitudes for it.
🔌

REST API

Everything the interface shows is served as JSON from /api/v1 with interactive OpenAPI docs — the seed of a future generic query builder for scripted access.
