What is BPASS?

The Binary Population and Spectral Synthesis code, by J. J. Eldridge (University of Auckland) and E. R. Stanway (University of Warwick), predicts what populations of stars look like as they age — their spectra, colours, ionizing output, supernova rates and chemical yields. Its defining feature is that it follows binary stellar evolution in detail: most massive stars have a companion close enough to interact, and those interactions reshape a population's light in ways single-star models miss. See the tutorial for a gentle introduction, or go straight to the official v2.2 user manual.

In this interface

Tutorial
What an SSP is, what the IMF and metallicity labels mean, and how binaries change the light.
SSP Explorer
Spectrum, star counts, supernova rates and ionizing output of one simple stellar population, from 1 Myr to 100 Gyr. A second population can be pinned for comparison. Photometry gives AB magnitudes in the bundled filters, or in a transmission curve you upload.
Fetch data
Download an SED, photometry, or the other population tables for a chosen IMF, metallicity and age.

Scripted or large-scale analysis

For work across the full BPASS grids — population synthesis, colour–magnitude diagrams, event rates — use hoki (Stevance, Eldridge & Stanway 2020), the BPASS manuals and the release papers. Install hoki with pip install hoki and cite it with those papers.
